Airworthiness Directives (ADs) are legally enforceable regulations issued by the FAA. They address unsafe conditions found in aircraft, engines, propellers, and appliances. 

If an AD applies to an aircraft you are buying, it **must** be complied with. If it hasn't been, the aircraft is technically not airworthy and cannot legally be flown.

### Why AD History Matters
When buying an aircraft, a pre-buy inspection is critical, but it doesn't always catch missing AD paperwork. An aircraft might look perfect, but if a repetitive AD (like a 100-hour inspection or a wing spar check) was skipped, you could be facing thousands of dollars in immediate maintenance costs just to make it legal to fly.

### How to Check ADs
1. **Find the Make and Model:** ADs are issued by manufacturer and model. 
2. **Check the Serial Number:** Many ADs only apply to specific serial number ranges. 
3. **Review the Logbooks:** The aircraft's maintenance logbooks must have a sign-off from an A&P mechanic or IA confirming the AD was complied with.
